Architecture

USB-C VBUS --> charger U3 (100 mA) --> +BATT --> regulator U5 --> 3.3 V --> ESP32-S3 module, clip flash U6
                                        |
                        cell + ---------+        cell - --> protection U4/Q1 --> GND

button --> ESP32-S3 <-- PDM microphone (powered from a GPIO)
           ESP32-S3 --> ADPCM clips on flash --> BLE --> phone app

The device sleeps until the button is pressed or USB is plugged in. Holding the button records; on release the clip is stored and the bookmark advertises for 30 s so the phone can fetch it. Protocol: docs/ble_protocol.md.

Ten 20-second clips and one sync a day is roughly 3.5 mAh, so a 150 mAh cell lasts about six weeks of daily use. Standby alone is limited by the cell's self-discharge, not the board.

Engineering decisions

TopicDecision
Module, not bare chipThe ESP32-S3-MINI-1 keeps the client's S3 firmware base and hardware PDM decoding, is 2.4 mm tall, and brings its radio certification to a 500-unit run. ESP32-C3/C6 cannot decode a PDM microphone in hardware; nRF52840 would mean rewriting working firmware.
No SD cardClips are IMA ADPCM (8 kB/s) on flash. That removes the tallest, most power-hungry prototype part and makes BLE sync four times faster than raw audio.
No power-path switchThe load sits on the cell, as on the XIAO. An OR-ing Schottky would leak more in standby than the rest of the board draws. Firmware sleeps when idle on USB so charging can terminate.
Pins chosen for the layoutEach signal group leaves the module on the side its parts sit on, so the top layer has no crossings. Only five signals use the back.
Height, not area, is tight0.8 mm board + 2.4 mm module + 2.5 mm cell = 5.7 mm of an assumed 6.0 mm. The USB-C connector is 3.3 mm tall, so the cell is kept clear of it.
Module sits 0.45 mm lower than its outline suggestsThe real module is 20.5 mm long; KiCad's footprint draws 20.1 mm. Placed so the real part stays on the board.
Microphone land pattern correctedThe imported footprint had a plated port hole and an unconnected four-piece ground ring. The hole is non-plated and the ring is tied to ground.

Open items

  • Enclosure CAD: every mechanical position (USB-C, button, microphone hole, LED, cell) is an assumption until it arrives.
  • Firmware does not use the 16 MB flash U6 yet; clips still go to the module's own flash (about 8.5 minutes).
  • Firmware compiles but has never run on hardware. The microphone's left/right slot setting is the likeliest first fix.
  • CPL rotations are not checked in JLCPCB's preview; Gerbers were not opened in a viewer.
  • The microphone 3D model is a same-size stand-in; compare the land pattern with the T3902 datasheet drawing.
  • Active-current and battery-life figures are estimates to be measured on the first boards.
